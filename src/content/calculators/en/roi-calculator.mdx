An ROI calculator measures how much an investment earned relative to its cost.
Enter the amount you put in and the amount you got back, and it returns the
return on investment as a percentage, the net profit in money, and — if you add
a holding period — the annualized ROI.

Plain ROI is great for a quick verdict, but it ignores time: a 50% return over
one year is far better than the same 50% over five. Annualized ROI fixes that by
restating the gain as a steady yearly rate, letting you compare very different
investments on equal terms.
